Memorization in Language Models

The notion of Memorization is developed by Carlini et. al.1

  • Extractability: A string is said to be extractable from a language model if that string is the most-likely-output by the model when queried using a prompt
  • k-Eidiatic Memorization: A string is said to be k-Eidiatic Memorized if it is extractable from the language model and has appeared as most k times in the data
  • With a larger number of parameters, memorization becomes more persistent
  • Memorization opens a door to manipulating these models to make inferences on training data
  • If the training data is private, then memorization can be exploited to launch privacy attacks

1Nicholas Carlini et al. “The Secret Sharer: Evaluating and Testing Unintended Memorization in Neural Networks.”. In: USENIX Security Symposium. Vol. 267. 2019.

Membership Inference Attack

  • In Membership-Inference-Attack3 the attacker has:
    • A classification model (target model) with black-box access (returns the classification accuracy)
    • Sample access to the distribution of the data used for training the target model
    • A single data point
  • The attacker's goal is to infer his data point is part of the training data of target model
  • Motivation
    • Curiosity!
    • The first step toward training data extraction attack
    • To cross-check whether a company has used his data
    • Auditing method, to some private algorithm

3Reza Shokri et al. “Membership inference attacks against machine learning models”. In: 2017 IEEE symposium on security and privacy (SP). IEEE. 2017, pp. 3–18.

Privacy Violation is different from Security Breach

As pointed out by Prof. Latanya Sweeney5

  • The area of authentication and access control might look similar to the privacy domain, but they are different!!
  • Works in the privacy domain assume that the recipient of the information has the authority to receive that information
  • Access control and authentication can safeguard against direct disclosures, but they do not address disclosures based on inferences made from released data
  • Privacy violations has legal, social, and technical aspects
  • One of the important tools for dealing with privacy violations is Differential Privacy

Differential Privacy: Motivation

  • Consider a database, containing the private responses of 100 students on their habit of taking tea
  • Consider two queries in the database:
    • What is the total number of students who do take tea?
    • What is the total number of students (excluding row 58) who do take tea?
  • With answers to these queries, analysts can find the private response of the student of row 58
  • This motivates us to obfuscate answers, before releasing, them so that both answers look identical
  • How much noise to add?
12 / 27

Quantification of Noise

One such method proposed, for obfuscating the answer was by Dwork et. al.6 The steps are as follows:

  1. List out all possible datasets that differ in exactly one row (also called neighboring datasets)
  2. Evaluate both the query over all possible neighboring datasets
  3. Calculate the maximum difference evaluated over all possible pairs of neighboring datasets (also called the sensitivity of the query)
  4. Calibrate the answer to the query before releasing it by adding noise proportional to the sensitivity

This technique was coined as Differential Privacy by the authors

6Cynthia Dwork et al. “Calibrating noise to sensitivity in private data analysis”. In: Theory of Cryptography: Third Theory of Cryptography Conference, TCC 2006, New York, NY, USA, March 4-7, 2006. Proceedings 3. Springer. 2006, pp. 265–284.

Differential Privacy (Formal Definition)

Here we will define the formal definition of Differential Privacy. Let:

  • 𝒟: Dataset containing personal information
  • 𝒟′: Dataset after discarding one row from 𝒟 (Neighboring dataset to 𝒟 denoted by 𝒟∼𝒟′)
  • 𝒜: An algorithm that receives the query from the user, evaluates the query, and, sends the obfuscated answer to the user
  • 𝒬: Set of all possible queries
  • 𝒴: Set of all possible outputs of 𝒜
  • ϵ: Privacy budget

Now the algorithm 𝒜 will be called a Differentially Private algorithm, with privacy budget ϵ if,

|log⁡[Pr[𝒜(𝒟,q)=y]Pr[𝒜(𝒟′,q)=y]]|≤ϵ,∀y∈𝒴,∀q∈𝒬,∀𝒟∼𝒟′

Relaxation Of Differential Privacy

Two relaxations of differential privacy have been proposed.

  • Approximate Differential Privacy
    • One additional parameter is introduced over Differential Privacy to capture the failure probability of the private mechanism; and tried to optimize it [6]13
    • Criterion: Pr[𝒜(𝒟,q)=y]≤eϵPr[𝒜(𝒟′,q)=y]+δ
  • Renyi Differential Privacy
    • This relaxation tries to address the above issue but with different parameterization Differential Privacy [9]14
    • Criterion: Pr[𝒜(𝒟,q)=y]≤(eϵPr[𝒜(𝒟′,q)=y])1−1α
