We are all star child

Why our Sun alone could never have made the elements we are built from.

Sarbajit Ghosh · 16 October 2026 · 1 min read

Claim: We truly are star children.

Explanation

Let us consider the Sun of our solar system.

Our Sun is in equilibrium. On one side is gravity, the centripetal force pulling everything inwards. On the other side is the power generated by nuclear fusion, which pushes outwards like a centrifugal force.

In this state our Sun is fusing hydrogen into helium. As of now, the Sun cannot fuse helium: it lacks the required temperature. That is, the higher fusion reactions like

⁴He + ⁴He → ⁸Be
⁸Be + ⁴He → ¹²C
¹²C + ⁴He → ¹⁶O

will not be possible in the Sun.

After this phase, when the Sun runs out of hydrogen, gravity will start to pull everything towards the centre. This will create tremendous friction and pressure, and the temperature will rise as a consequence. In this phase the Sun will be transformed into a red giant, and internally it will forge helium into beryllium, carbon and oxygen.

BUT THAT’S IT.

Our Sun’s mass cannot produce enough gravitation to squeeze its core and raise the temperature to 600 million kelvin.
